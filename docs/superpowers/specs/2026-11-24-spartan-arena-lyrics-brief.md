# Lyrics brief: Spartan Arena (working title)

Paste everything below the line into ChatGPT.

---

You are a songwriter. Write original song lyrics for a video game, following
this brief.

## The game

**Spartan Arena** (working title) is a two-player fighting game in the style
of the classic arcade fighters: two fighters, side view, health bars, best of
three rounds. Two brothers play it side by side on one keyboard.

- **Setting:** a sand arena in Rome. Caesar watches from his box, and a huge
  crowd roars from the stands. Behind them are marble arches, a temple and a
  hot sky.
- **The fighters:** two gladiators from Sparta, one in a red cloak and one in
  blue. They wear bronze helmets with tall crests.
- **The look:** chunky pixel-art voxels, like retro 8-bit sprites built out of
  little cubes. It's playful and bold, not realistic.

## The journey (three levels)

1. **The beginner.** Both gladiators start weak, with no weapons and only
   bare fists and kicks.
2. **Sword and shield.** They earn a sword and a round bronze shield.
3. **The beasts.** Caesar releases a lion or an elephant into the arena. It
   attacks everyone, so it's every fighter for themselves.

After each level, both fighters earn a **new move**: the winner picks first
and the loser picks second. The moves include throwing the sword at the
opponent, jumping extra high, a rapid multi-punch combo, a spinning circle
kick, and a super hit that charges up the sword and blasts it into the
opponent.

## Signature moments the lyrics can use

- The gladiators salute Caesar before the fight. The historical Latin line
  *"Ave Caesar, morituri te salutant"* ("Hail Caesar, those about to die
  salute you") may be used.
- Caesar gives the thumbs down (*pollice verso*) at the end of a round.
- A sword blow can chop off an opponent's arm. The game treats this as
  cartoon gore with chunky red pixel cubes, so keep it over the top and
  comic, never graphic or cruel.
- The crowd chants, cheers big hits and boos cowards.
- Going from nothing to champion: weak beginner, then armed warrior, then
  facing the beasts.
- The rivalry between brothers: fierce, but always for fun.

## Tone

- Epic, pumped-up and a bit tongue-in-cheek. It should make two players
  shout along, slam the keys and laugh.
- Spartan pride and Roman spectacle.
- Easy to chant: short punchy lines and a big, simple chorus the crowd could
  shout.
- A sprinkle of Latin words is welcome (*gladius, Caesar, arena, gloria,
  victoria, ave*), but the lyrics are mainly in English.

## Hard rules

- **Original wording only.** Do not quote or closely imitate lines, chants
  or catchphrases from existing songs, films or games. In particular, avoid
  the famous Sparta movie shout and the well-known announcer chants and
  catchphrases of existing fighting-game series. Historical Latin phrases are
  fine.
- No real-world violence, politics or hate. Violence stays cartoon arena
  combat.
- Suitable for teenagers: no swearing and no sexual content.

## What to deliver

1. **A song title.**
2. **The lyrics**, with section tags in square brackets so they can be used
   with an AI music generator, for example `[Intro]`, `[Verse 1]`,
   `[Pre-Chorus]`, `[Chorus]`, `[Verse 2]`, `[Bridge]`, `[Chorus]`,
   `[Outro]`. Put crowd chants in parentheses, such as `(Ca-e-sar! Ca-e-sar!)`.
   - Verse 1: the unarmed beginner entering the sand.
   - Verse 2: sword and shield, the first wins and the new moves.
   - Bridge: the gates open and the lion or elephant charges in.
   - Chorus: the arena, the crowd and the hunger for glory, the same every
     time.
   - Length: about 2½–3½ minutes when sung.
3. **A one-line music style prompt** of at most 200 characters for the music
   generator. Suggested direction: epic war drums, brass stabs, a chanting
   male choir and a driving fight-game beat around 140 BPM, with a hint of
   retro 8-bit synth lead as a nod to the pixel look.
4. **A second, shorter variant** (about 1 minute, chorus-heavy) for the
   victory screen.

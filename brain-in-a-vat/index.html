About the thought experiment

Suppose a brain is removed from its body, kept alive in a vat of nutrient fluid, and wired to a computer that feeds it exactly the signals it would receive from a body walking around in the world. From the inside, nothing would seem different. So how do you know this is not your situation right now?

It is a modern version of Descartes' evil demon (1641). Gilbert Harman described the wired-up brain in 1973, and Hilary Putnam made it famous in Reason, Truth and History (1981), where he argued that the sentence "I am a brain in a vat" cannot be true when a vat-brain says it, because its word "vat" has only ever been connected to simulated vats.

The pictures here are illustrations, not anatomy: the folds are procedural noise, and no real brain can be kept alive like this.

Marble generator

Marble is limestone that was buried, heated and squeezed until its calcite recrystallised. Whatever else was in the sediment, clay, iron, graphite or serpentine, was smeared into flowing bands, and later cracks were filled with new minerals. This page builds a slab from those parts: a cloudy ground, a few big flowing veins, a finer network, hairline cracks, broken clasts in a matrix, pores, and a polished surface.

Recrystallised limestone

Limestone is made of calcite, mostly from shells. Buried kilometres deep and heated to several hundred degrees, its grains dissolve and regrow as larger interlocking crystals, and the fossils and bedding are wiped out. Pure calcite marble is white, as in Carrara or Thassos. The sugary sparkle of a broken or honed surface is those crystals; “Grain” on this page imitates it.

Where the veins come from

Colour comes from impurities. Clay and mica give grey, graphite gives grey to black, iron oxides yellow, orange and red, serpentine and chlorite green. While hot, the rock deforms like very stiff toffee, so impurity layers are stretched into bands, folded, and pulled into lenses (“pinch and swell”). Cracks that open later fill with new calcite, which is why dark stones carry white veins.

Breccia, travertine and “green marble”

Where rock was shattered, as along a fault, angular fragments (clasts) are cemented again by a finer matrix: a breccia, as in Arabescato or Paonazzo. Travertine is not marble at all but limestone laid down by hot springs, full of holes from gas and plants. Many green “marbles” are serpentinite, a rock from the mantle, crossed by white calcite veins.

What comes from the real thing, and what this page chose

From the real thing

  • Veins follow a direction of flow and are bent, folded and pulled thin and thick along their length.
  • Big veins are often a soft cloudy band with darker thin lines at their edges, and fibres along their length.
  • Fine hairline cracks run across everything, often in a different colour from the main veins.
  • Breccia is angular clasts, themselves cracked, floating in a matrix that widens where the rock was most broken.
  • Calcite is translucent: light goes a few millimetres into the stone, which softens the veins and gives marble its glow.
  • Travertine pores are stretched along the layers.

Chosen by this page

  • Nothing here simulates heat, pressure or flow. The veins are lines in a bent noise field, chosen because they look right, not grown.
  • Translucency is a blurred copy of each vein mixed under the sharp one, not light transport.
  • Colours were picked by eye from photographs. Presets are named after the look of well-known stones; they are not portraits of a particular quarry.
  • With Seamless on, the veins run level or upright. A slanted pattern can only wrap by repeating itself inside the tile, so turn the texture where you use it instead.
  • The light is one direction with a simple highlight and an optional window reflection. It is a texture preview, not a physically based render.

Modes & banking

R15 bits 1:0

Four modes, and each one swaps in its own copy of the high registers. That is why an interrupt handler needs no stack to get started.

FIQ banks R8 to R14, so a fast handler has seven private registers and never has to save anything. IRQ and SVC bank only R13 and R14.

The ARM1 had no display hardware at all — it was a prototype on a board that plugged into a BBC Micro. This framebuffer and the timer are invented here so the demos have somewhere to draw. One byte per pixel, three bits of red, three of green, two of blue, no palette and no interleaving.

04 / Registers

Banked registers in orange

R15 is the program counter and the status register in one word: flags at the top, mode at the bottom, and 24 bits of word address in between. Reading it gives this instruction's address plus 8, because two more are already in the pipeline.

An ARM immediate is an 8-bit value rotated right by an even number of places. Most 32-bit constants cannot be written as one, so use LDR R0, =value and the assembler puts the word in a literal pool for you. POOL places that pool; otherwise it goes at the end.

A label named DONE gets a debugger breakpoint automatically.

Model & limitations

This is ARMv1, which is what the 1985 silicon ran: sixteen data-processing operations, B and BL, LDR and STR, LDM and STM, and SWI. That is the entire instruction set. There is no MUL — multiply arrived with ARM2 — no SWP, no coprocessor instructions, no MRS or MSR, and no MMU, because on an Archimedes that was a separate chip called MEMC.

Every instruction is conditional and every operand 2 goes through the barrel shifter, including the LSR #0, ASR #0 and RRX forms and the shifter's carry-out. R15 holds the flags, the interrupt disables, a 24-bit word program counter and the mode, all in one register. Reading it gives the instruction's address plus 8, or plus 12 for the shifted register when the shift amount comes from another register; storing it gives plus 12. Those are pipeline artefacts and they are modelled.

Memory is 1 MB of RAM from zero. An address beyond 26 bits raises a real address exception through the vector at &14, which is genuine ARM1 behaviour that disappeared after ARM2. Prefetch and data aborts are never raised, because nothing here can abort a transfer.

The framebuffer at &100000 and the timer at &200000 are this page's inventions, not Acorn hardware. The ARM1 had no display of its own; it lived on an evaluation board plugged into a BBC Micro, and it was the ARM2 that reached the Archimedes. Nothing else answers: unmapped reads return zero.

Instruction-level emulation. The clock advances by a count of memory cycles per instruction, which is how the ARM datasheets describe timing, but no bus contention or memory speed is modelled. No firmware sets anything up, so this page does: R13 starts at &10000 for user and supervisor, &F000 for IRQ and &E000 for FIQ so the banks do not share a stack, the mode is SVC, and execution begins at the first ORG rather than at the reset vector.

Keyboard: Space run/pause, S step, B back, R reset. Shortcuts are inactive inside controls. Back undoes up to 512 instructions.

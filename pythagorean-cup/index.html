The trick, step by step
  1. The hidden pipe: two little holes near the floor feed a channel that climbs the central column, bends over at a secret height, then dives down through the base.
  2. Climbing while you pour: connected vessels! Liquid rises inside the pipe exactly level with the drink. Harmless… so far.
  3. Over the bend: pass the critical line and the liquid crests the bend and plunges down the far side, out of the base.
  4. Siphon lock: the falling column seals the pipe. Gravity tugs the whole slug downward, lowering its pressure — so air pressure on the drink shoves more liquid up the pipe to replace it. Same reason a straw works.
  5. The glug: only when the cup is empty does air break in through the intake holes. Not a drop is left for you.

Said to be invented by Pythagoras of Samos (~530 BC) to ration wine: take your fair share, or leave with nothing.

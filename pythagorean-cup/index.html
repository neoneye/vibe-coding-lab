How it works
  1. A hidden pipe runs up through the middle of the cup and down through its base, opening just below a secret line.
  2. Pour normally and nothing happens — the liquid sits safely under the opening.
  3. Cross the line and liquid spills over the pipe’s lip, falls down the channel and out of the bottom.
  4. Falling liquid creates a siphon: gravity pulling the column down lowers the pressure behind it, so atmospheric pressure keeps pushing the rest of the cup over the lip. The cup empties itself, right to the last drop.

Said to be invented by Pythagoras of Samos (~530 BC) to ration wine — take only your fair share, or you get none at all.

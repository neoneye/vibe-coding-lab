How to scan

  1. Sit on a swivel chair (or stand) with your head and shoulders in view, good light, plain background helps.
  2. Face the camera and hold still for a second.
  3. Turn slowly, about 20° a second, in either direction. Two full turns, ending facing the camera.
  4. The head is rebuilt and shown in 3D.

How it works

MediaPipe's pose model tracks how far you have turned from your shoulders, ears and nose, and every time your face looks straight at the camera the count is pinned to 0°, 360° or 720°. Its multiclass segmenter cuts out hair, face and neck (head) or the whole person (body) in every frame; a body is centred on the pose model's hip and shoulder midline. The steadiest frame per 2° of each turn is kept, so every angle is seen twice. Space carving keeps the voxels that (almost) every silhouette agrees on, surface nets turn them into a mesh, and each vertex takes its colour from the photos that look at it most squarely.

Limits: silhouettes cannot see hollows (eye sockets, armpits and the gap between the legs fill in), so keep arms a little away from the body. Head size assumes a 63 mm eye distance, body size comes from MediaPipe's metric landmarks; the camera is treated as orthographic. Nothing leaves the browser.

Retro GUIs: Project ORCHID

Six desktops of 1996, each on its own machine on the same network: Windows 95, OS/2 Warp 4, X11 with twm, BeOS on a BeBox, Amiga Workbench 3.1 and an Amiga with MUI (the Magic User Interface). Windows drag, resize, minimise and stack the way each system did it: the Amiga's menus live in the screen title bar while you hold the right mouse button, BeOS windows wear yellow tabs, twm iconifies to the root window, OS/2 has its WarpCenter, Windows its Start menu.

The Amiga and X11 text uses pixel fonts that the page draws and compiles into real TrueType files when it loads. Icons are 16×16 pixel art, recoloured per system: four colours on Workbench, the MagicWB eight on MUI, black and white on X11.

The story

October 1996. You are a contractor at Halyard Systems, makers of the WARDEN security product. Your colleague M. found something called ORCHID inside it, mailed you a disk, and disappeared. Her records are gone. Start on the Windows 95 desk: read the Inbox, look in the Recycle Bin, then use the Network Neighborhood (or the buttons above) to visit the other machines.

  • The shells are real little interpreters: dir, type, cd on the PCs; ls, cat, finger, last on orion; DIR, TYPE, INFO on the Amigas. telnet moves between machines.
  • On the BeBox, listen to the 3 a.m. recording, and watch it.
  • The disk is an Amiga disk. The decoder needs MUI.

Notes

Everything is fictional: Halyard, WARDEN, ORCHID, Northline Air and every person. The look of each desktop is redrawn from memory, with no original artwork, logos or fonts. Your name stays in this browser.
